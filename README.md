# Mechanical Design & Development

**MREN 403 | Autonomous Snow-Clearing Robot for Kingston City Sidewalks**

This repository contains the mechanical engineering design and development of the autonomous snow-clearing robot being developed as part of the MREN 403 capstone project at Queen's University.

The mechanical subsystem focuses on the design, analysis, and integration of the robot's physical structure, drivetrain, and snow-clearing mechanism. This repository serves as the central location for mechanical CAD models, engineering drawings, design calculations, and concept development throughout the project.

---

## 1. Mechanical System Overview

The mechanical design is focused on developing a compact, mobile robotic platform capable of navigating pedestrian sidewalks and performing snow-clearing operations.

The primary mechanical design objectives include:

- **Chassis Design:** Develop a structural platform capable of supporting the robot's mechanical and electrical components.
- **Drivetrain:** Design a suitable drive system providing sufficient traction, mobility, and manoeuvrability on winter sidewalk surfaces.
- **Snow-Clearing Mechanism:** Develop a mechanical system capable of removing accumulated snow from pedestrian pathways.
- **Component Integration:** Ensure compatibility between mechanical, electrical, and software subsystems.
- **Structural Integrity:** Evaluate mechanical components under expected operating loads and environmental conditions.
- **Manufacturability:** Prioritize practical manufacturing methods, material selection, accessibility, and ease of assembly.

### Design Calculations

Engineering calculations will be documented and organized according to the mechanical subsystem being evaluated.

Potential areas of analysis include:
- Motor and drivetrain sizing.
- Torque and power requirements.
- Wheel selection and traction.
- Structural loading and material selection.
- Snow-clearing mechanism sizing.
- Shaft, bearing, and fastener selection.
- Mechanical factor of safety.

Calculations will include relevant assumptions, governing equations, supporting references, and design conclusions.

## 3. Design Development

The mechanical design will progress through several stages:

1. **Concept Development:** Identify design requirements, investigate potential mechanisms, and develop preliminary concepts.
2. **Preliminary Design:** Establish system geometry, component selection, and initial mechanical configurations.
3. **Detailed Design:** Develop complete SolidWorks models, assemblies, and engineering drawings.
4. **Engineering Analysis:** Perform calculations and evaluate mechanical performance against established requirements.
5. **Integration:** Coordinate mechanical interfaces with electrical and software subsystems.
6. **Prototype & Validation:** Manufacture, assemble, and evaluate the mechanical system through physical testing.

Design files and supporting documentation will be updated as the project progresses.

---

## 4. CAD File Conventions

To maintain consistency and simplify collaboration, CAD files should follow a standardized naming convention.

Suggested format:

`[Subsystem]_[Component]_[Version]`

Examples:
- `Chassis_MainFrame_V01.SLDPRT`
- `Drivetrain_WheelAssembly_V01.SLDASM`
- `SnowClearing_Auger_V01.SLDPRT`

Where applicable:
- Use descriptive component names.
- Keep assembly references and associated part files together.
- Include engineering drawings for finalized components.
- Avoid committing temporary or automatically generated SolidWorks files.
